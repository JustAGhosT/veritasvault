The Problem

Your mandate does not fit on-chain infrastructure

Digital-asset yields are real. The operational apparatus a regulated institution needs to capture them is not.

I

Allocation by intuition

Most on-chain treasury allocation is discretionary or naively yield-chasing. There is no equilibrium anchor, no formal expression of house view, and no way to show a committee why the portfolio is weighted as it is.

II

Risk measured after the fact

Protocol risk, counterparty exposure and drawdown are reconstructed from block explorers and spreadsheets once something has already gone wrong, rather than enforced as limits at the point of allocation.

III

Evidence assembled retrospectively

When an auditor, regulator or investment committee asks why a position was taken, the answer is reconstructed from chat logs and memory — not produced from a system designed to record it.

What this costs you

Capital sits on the sidelines, not because the opportunity is unattractive, but because the operating model cannot be defended to the people who must approve it. The constraint is governance, not conviction.

What a mandate actually requires

  • A defensible allocation method with an economic rationale
  • Constraints enforced, not merely monitored
  • Attribution — which decision produced which exposure
  • An audit trail produced as a by-product of operating, not assembled later

The Allocation Engine

Black-Litterman, applied to digital assets

The allocation framework institutional asset management already trusts — brought to on-chain portfolios without abandoning its theoretical foundation. Specified in full; in build now.

1 · Market equilibrium
Start from the returns implied by market-capitalisation weights — what the market as a whole already believes, rather than a forecast built from nothing.
→
2 · Your views
Express house views as absolute or relative statements, each with a confidence level, a written rationale and supporting evidence.
→
3 · Posterior allocation
Bayesian blending produces posterior returns and optimal weights — tilted toward your views in proportion to your confidence, and no further.
Why it matters for a mandate
  • No unexplained concentration. Weights derive from equilibrium plus stated views, so extreme allocations cannot appear without a view to justify them.
  • Confidence is explicit. A weakly held view moves the portfolio weakly. Conviction is a parameter, not an argument.
  • Constraints are first-class. Mandate limits, exposure bounds and asset restrictions enter the optimisation directly.
  • Results are interpretable. Every output is economically meaningful — a requirement for committee approval and regulatory explanation.

Enhanced where estimation is weakest

The model's most fragile input is the covariance matrix. Our specification applies machine-learning shrinkage estimation — evaluated against Frobenius, KL-divergence and spectral loss, with ensemble architecture and explicit uncertainty quantification — to reduce estimation noise without disturbing the Bayesian framework.

AI that degrades gracefully

AI assists parameter estimation, view suggestion and confidence calibration. Every component is explainable, gated by validation, and optional: the platform remains fully functional with AI enhancement switched off.

Attribution & Audit

Every tilt traceable to a named view

Auditability is not a reporting layer bolted on afterwards. It is the shape of the data model itself.

What a view records

RationaleWritten justification, stored with the view
Supporting evidenceReferences backing the position taken
Author & timestampWho formed the view, and when
ConfidenceExplicit weight given to the view
ExpiryViews lapse rather than persisting silently

What the result reports back

Impact scoreHow much each view moved the portfolio
Weight changesPer-asset delta attributable to that view
Return contributionExpected return traced to the view
Risk contributionRisk added or removed by the view
Tilt magnitudeTotal divergence from market equilibrium
The question this answers

“Why does the portfolio hold this position?” is answerable in one step — naming the view, its author, its rationale, its confidence, and the precise weight change it produced. That is the difference between a portfolio you can operate and one you can defend.
