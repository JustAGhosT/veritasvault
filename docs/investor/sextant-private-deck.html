The Honest Starting Point

Most digital-asset portfolios are allocated by feel

Not through carelessness — through the absence of any practical alternative at individual scale.

I

Position sizes chosen by instinct

You hold a conviction about an asset. Translating that conviction into a defensible position size — relative to everything else you own — is guesswork. So the number is usually round, and usually arbitrary.

II

Yield chased, risk inferred

Pools get compared on headline yield because that figure is visible. Protocol risk, concentration and correlation are what actually determine the outcome — and they are the figures nobody shows you.

III

No record of why

Six months on you cannot reconstruct why you took a position, what you believed at the time, or whether the thesis held. Without that record, there is nothing to learn from.

The gap

Institutions solved this decades ago. They do not allocate by instinct — they start from what the market already implies, add their own views with explicit confidence, and record both. The method exists. It has simply never been available at your scale.

What changes with an instrument

  • Position sizes follow from your views, not from round numbers
  • Conviction becomes a number you set, not a feeling you defend
  • Risk is a constraint applied up front, not a lesson learned after
  • Every decision leaves a record you can review honestly

The Method

Take a reading. Then set your course.

A sextant does not tell you where to sail. It tells you precisely where you are, by measuring against references that do not move. Everything after that is your decision. This is the method we are building to — see the status page for exactly where it stands.

STEP ONE
Fix your position
Rather than forecasting from a blank page, we begin from the returns the whole market's positioning already implies — a neutral, defensible reference point that requires no prediction from you.
→
STEP TWO
Declare your bearing
State your views directly — “this asset will outperform that one”, or “this one returns 8%”. For each, you set how confident you are, and you write down why.
→
STEP THREE
Sail the blended course
The Black-Litterman model combines both mathematically. Your portfolio tilts toward your views in proportion to your confidence — decisively if you are certain, barely if you are not.

Why this beats picking numbers

Conventional portfolio optimisation is notoriously unstable — a small change in a return estimate can swing the whole allocation into absurd concentration. Black-Litterman anchors to market equilibrium, so weights stay sensible, and an extreme position can only appear if you have explicitly stated a view that justifies it.

The instrument never overrules you

Confidence is your input, not the model's. A view you hold loosely moves the portfolio slightly; one you hold firmly moves it decisively. Sextant does not decide how much you believe something — it works out what that belief implies for your weights.

Your Views

Your conviction, written down and measured

Each view is a record. The portfolio that results is fully attributable back to those records.

What you record when you form a view

The viewAbsolute (“this returns 8%”) or relative (“this beats that”)
ConfidenceHow strongly you hold it — this sets its influence
RationaleWhy you believe it, in your own words
EvidenceWhat you are basing it on
ExpiryWhen it should lapse if untested

What Sextant tells you back

ImpactHow much this view actually moved your portfolio
Weight changeExactly which holdings shifted, and by how much
Return effectExpected return attributable to this view
Risk effectRisk this view added — or removed
Total tiltHow far you now sit from a neutral market position
Why this matters more than it sounds

Most investors cannot separate a good decision from a lucky one. When every position traces back to a dated, written, confidence-weighted view, you can finally review your own judgement honestly — which views paid, which did not, and whether your confidence was calibrated. That is the advantage that compounds.
